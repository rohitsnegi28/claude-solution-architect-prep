# Glossary

The expanded source glossary lives in `reference/glossary.md`. This root glossary keeps the highest-frequency revision terms.

| Term | Simple Meaning | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI Fluency | The ability to collaborate with AI effectively and responsibly. | Core frame for the course. |
| Automation | AI performs a clear task. | Best for repeatable work with clear success criteria. |
| Augmentation | AI supports human thinking. | Best for creative, analytical, or exploratory work. |
| Agency | AI acts more independently toward a goal. | Powerful but requires stronger boundaries and oversight. |
| Delegation | Deciding which work belongs to humans, AI, or both. | Prevents unsafe or ineffective AI use. |
| Description | Giving AI clear context, instructions, constraints, and output expectations. | Improves quality and reduces ambiguity. |
| Discernment | Evaluating AI output, process, and behavior. | Protects against errors and weak reasoning. |
| Diligence | Responsible, transparent, and accountable AI use. | Connects AI work to ethics, safety, and governance. |
| Generative AI | AI that creates new content. | Distinguishes Claude-like systems from traditional AI. |
| LLM | A language model that understands and generates text. | Core technology behind Claude-like assistants. |
| Context Window | The amount of information a model can consider at once. | Affects long-document and memory-heavy tasks. |
| Hallucination | A confident but incorrect or unsupported AI answer. | Requires validation before use. |
| Knowledge Cutoff | The date after which a model may not know new facts. | Matters for current or changing information. |
| Accountability | Human responsibility for how AI output is used. | AI assistance does not transfer responsibility away from the user. |
