# Lesson 10: Diligence

## Topic Overview

Diligence is the AI Fluency competency focused on ethical and safety responsibilities in AI collaboration. It reminds users that effective AI use is not only about speed or efficiency. Users must also consider privacy, transparency, fairness, bias, accountability, and the broader impact of AI-assisted work.

The main idea is simple: even when AI contributes to the work, the human user remains responsible for how the system is chosen, how AI involvement is disclosed, and how the final output is used.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, I should be able to:

- Define diligence in the AI Fluency framework.
- Explain creation, transparency, and deployment diligence.
- Identify ethical and safety questions to ask before using AI.
- Explain why users remain accountable for AI-assisted outputs.
- Connect diligence to trust, governance, privacy, and responsible AI use.

## Key Concepts

### Diligence

Diligence means using AI responsibly, safely, transparently, and accountably. It requires users to think beyond productivity and consider whether their AI use is appropriate, fair, privacy-preserving, and trustworthy.

### Legal and Regulatory Uncertainty

AI law and regulation are still evolving. Because formal rules may not always be clear, users should develop personal and organizational guidelines for responsible AI use.

## Three Pillars of Diligence

### 1. Creation Diligence

Creation diligence means being critical and intentional about which AI systems you use and how you use them.

Important questions:

- How was the AI system trained?
- Who owns or controls the data?
- What happens to the data I provide?
- Is private or sensitive information protected?
- Is this AI system appropriate for the task?

Exam idea: Choosing an AI system is part of responsible AI use, not just a technical preference.

### 2. Transparency Diligence

Transparency diligence means being open and honest about when and how AI contributed to your work.

This matters in:

- Professional communication.
- Academic work.
- Business deliverables.
- Customer-facing outputs.
- Team collaboration.

Exam idea: Transparency builds trust because others can understand the role AI played in the final output.

### 3. Deployment Diligence

Deployment diligence means taking personal responsibility for AI-generated outputs before using or sharing them.

It includes:

- Verifying facts.
- Checking for bias.
- Reviewing quality.
- Ensuring the output meets the required standard.
- Owning the final result as if you created it yourself.

Exam idea: AI assistance does not remove human accountability.

## Architecture / Solution Design Connection

For solution architects, diligence maps directly to responsible AI governance.

A well-designed AI solution should consider:

- Data privacy and data handling rules.
- Whether AI usage should be disclosed to users.
- Bias and fairness checks.
- Output verification workflows.
- Auditability and accountability.
- Human approval for high-risk use cases.

## Exam-Focused Takeaways

- Diligence is about responsibility, ethics, safety, and accountability.
- Creation diligence asks whether the chosen AI system and data practices are appropriate.
- Transparency diligence asks whether AI involvement should be disclosed.
- Deployment diligence asks whether the final output is verified and safe to use.
- Users remain responsible for the final work even when AI helped create it.
- Diligence is especially important when legal and regulatory guidance is still evolving.

## Likely Questions

## Short Answers

1. Diligence is the responsible, ethical, safe, transparent, and accountable use of AI.
2. The three pillars are creation diligence, transparency diligence, and deployment diligence.
3. Creation diligence means intentionally choosing AI systems and understanding data ownership, training, privacy, and system suitability.
4. Transparency builds trust by making it clear when and how AI contributed to the work.
5. Deployment diligence requires verifying facts, checking bias, reviewing quality, and taking responsibility for the final output.
6. The user decides how AI output is used, so the user remains accountable for the impact of the final work.
7. Solution architects apply diligence through privacy controls, disclosure patterns, validation workflows, governance, audits, and human oversight.

## Quick Revision Notes

- Diligence = responsible AI use.
- Creation = choose AI systems carefully.
- Transparency = disclose AI involvement when appropriate.
- Deployment = verify and own the final output.
- AI can assist, but accountability stays human.
